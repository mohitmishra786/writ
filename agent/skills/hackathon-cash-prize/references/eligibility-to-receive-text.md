# Eligibility to receive: the text you publish

## The split, and published precedent for it

Eligibility to **compete** answers who may enter and build. Eligibility to **receive** answers who may take home the award once a rules-eligible team has won. They are different permissions and most published rule sets conflate them.

Two sources draw the line explicitly, from opposite sides of the guest list. MLH's copy-paste rule set states: "This event is limited to students. No volunteers, organizers, or mentors are eligible to win any prizes, though they can work on projects for their portfolio if they wish." That single sentence grants permission to build and withholds permission to win, which is the split in published form.

A second source draws the same line around sponsor and host staff rather than volunteers. The Gemma 4 Hackathon (Paris, an in-person event run on Kaggle's competition infrastructure) states in its official rules: "Employees, interns, contractors, officers, directors, judges, and organizers of the Competition Entities may participate only if permitted by their applicable internal policies, but they are not eligible to receive Prizes." Its "Competition Entities" definition folds together the host, the prize-funding sponsors, and their own organizers and judges, so one clause covers both the volunteer-side exclusion MLH names and the sponsor-employee exclusion MLH leaves blank.

A third MLH document closes part of the remaining gap at the shape level. MLH's Contest Terms and Conditions is the document every registrant agrees to, separate from and more detailed than the one-sentence staff exclusion in its copy-paste rules. It states three things:

- a real age-and-permission condition for minors
- a bar on a sponsor's own employees, contractors and household members competing at all, which is a blanket exclusion rather than the compete-but-not-receive split used for volunteers
- that tax responsibility for a prize shifts to the winner rather than the organizer, with a claim step and a tax-identification form collected after the win

None of the specifics transfer. The age condition and the identity-collection mechanic are exactly the kind of jurisdiction-specific figure and form name this skill has committed never to state. The shape does transfer: a published convention exists for all three categories, not only for people working the event, so an organizer drafting from a blank page is not the first to have answered these questions.

Two questions stay genuinely open even so: who receives or countersigns a minor's prize on the minor's behalf, and whether the payment rail can reach a given country at all.

Limits on how far these precedents reach:

- MLH's copy-paste rules cover one category only: people working the event. Its separate Contest Terms document reaches minors and cross-border winners too, but at the shape level only.
- MLH itself flags that its copy-paste set deliberately leaves age and demographic conditions blank for the organizer to fill.
- The Gemma 4 Hackathon source folds sponsor employees into the same clause only because its "Competition Entities" definition already named the funding sponsors as parties to the rules. A sponsorship structured without that shared-rules mechanism needs its own equivalent clause, not a borrowed one.

Beyond the split itself, the framing is a policy you set rather than a convention you can cite. Present it that way.

`samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-volunteer-experience` arrives at the same conclusion from the other side, deleting a volunteer reward with real cash value on the grounds that a reward reading as wages stops the arrangement being volunteering. Two skills, two directions, one principle: people working the event sit outside the receiving pool whatever they are allowed to build.

## The carve-out list

Work through every row before writing the paragraph. Each row is a fact to establish, not a rule this skill can supply.

| Category                                          | The question to answer                                           | Where the answer comes from                              |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Organizers, volunteers, mentors, judges           | May they build? May they win?                                    | Published practice exists (above); the organizer decides |
| Minors                                            | Who receives or countersigns on the winner's behalf              | Counsel or the fiscal host - jurisdiction-specific       |
| Winners outside the organizer's country           | Whether the chosen medium can reach them at all                  | The payment rail, the fiscal host, or the sponsor        |
| Sponsor employees                                 | May they win the prize their own employer funded                 | The signed sponsorship agreement, then counsel           |
| Team members who did not attend                   | Does the award follow the submitted roster or the people present | The organizer, published in advance                      |
| Winners who cannot satisfy a redemption condition | Expiry, region, account requirement on a credit or voucher       | The organizer, published in advance                      |

## A publishable paragraph

Adapt, do not copy verbatim. Hand the result to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@hackathon-brief-design` for the rules document rather than publishing it yourself.

> Prizes are awarded to the team named in the submission. Organizers, volunteers, mentors and judges may build projects and demo them, and are not eligible to receive a prize. [Sponsor employees may / may not receive a prize funded by their own employer.] A winner under the age of majority in [place] will be asked to have a parent or guardian receive the prize on their behalf. Where a prize cannot be delivered to a winner's location, we will agree an alternative of comparable value with that winner. Prizes are awarded per team member up to the team-size limit stated above; unclaimed units are [returned / raffled / held]. To receive a prize you will be asked to confirm your identity and any information we are required to collect; we will tell you exactly what that is when you claim.

The bracketed fields are the ones an organizer must actually decide. Publishing the paragraph with a bracket still in it is worse than publishing nothing, because it advertises that the question was seen and skipped.

## A wording pair

**Do:** "Organizers, volunteers, mentors and judges may build projects and demo them, and are not eligible to receive a prize." It grants and withholds separately, so nobody has to infer which one was meant.

**Do not:** "Organizers and volunteers may not participate." It is ambiguous between "may not build" and "may not win", it reads as excluding people from their own event, and it silently contradicts the practice of letting the team build something for their portfolio.

## Conditional and revocable awards

An award can depend on something the winner keeps doing. MLH's published rules make prize eligibility conditional on the submission staying public: "Your code must remain public post event to be eligible for prizes. If your repo and video are not public, new winners may be selected."

Where you adopt a continuing condition, publish two things alongside it:

- the date the award becomes final
- what happens to the prize if the condition fails after the handover

A condition with no stated end leaves every award permanently revocable. Nobody intends that, and it costs far more on a cash prize than on a trophy.

Reversal after a payout is materially harder than reversal before one. `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@hackathon-judging` closes its own contest window before prizes change hands for exactly this reason. Keep that ordering.
