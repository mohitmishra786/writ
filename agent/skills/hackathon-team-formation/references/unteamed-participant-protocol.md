## Backstop 1 - solo, said out loud

"A project of one, meaning someone working alone, is okay too!"

Say this at the opening, before the mechanic runs. It is the cheapest thing on this page and it converts a whole category of outcome from failure into choice - for the people who wanted it. It answers nothing for anyone else, which is why it is deleted as the _whole_ answer the moment you publish a mechanic that implies people will be teamed.

## Backstop 2 - the stocked backlog

"have ideas for new projects that are especially easy for participants to get started with if they can't join an existing project. Having project ideas ready is especially important if you do not expect many participants to bring projects! And always be open to project ideas from participants."

This is the mitigation that reduces how often anyone is unteamed at all, which is why it sits below the sweep on value but above it on effort. Its bar, from the same source's project checklist:

- **"ready-to-go tasks for newcomers with a variety of skills and at a variety of skill levels"**
- coding tasks that do not require intimate knowledge of the codebase
- a build environment that **"can be spun up in less than 20 minutes"**

A listed idea that fails that bar is not a backstop; it is a line of text.

Size it against the themed-event ratio if the event is themed - one subject-matter expert plus workable project for about every four non-expert participants, with every one of that number's bounds carried (see the sizing reference). At an open-theme event no ratio exists and the count is your own.

## Backstop 3 - the floor sweep

"Have someone managing the hacking room. Go around to check that every project is going smoothly. See if anyone needs anything or can't find something to work on."

That is the role and the question. The owner, the cadence and the offer are yours to fix:

- **Owner.** One named person for the window, not "an organizer". `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-volunteers` rosters them; this skill supplies the constraint that the role must exist and must be continuously staffed rather than covered by whoever is free.
- **Cadence.** Anchored to the day's own clock, not to a stopwatch. The sweep is a continuous round, and the useful passes are the ones already implied by the run of show: once when the mechanic ends, once at the first meal, once at the first workshop break.
- **The offer.** Three routes, in this order, and the order is the point: a team known to be short-handed, a stocked project, and solo work presented as a choice. Leading with solo is the failure mode; it is the right sentence in the wrong position.

The sweeper also needs one input nobody generates automatically: **which teams have room.** The published size rule makes that computable - a team of two under a cap of four has room - but only if somebody wrote the rosters down when the mechanic ran. Capture them at that moment or the sweep has nothing to route toward.

## The escalation

Nothing published describes an escalation for an unteamed participant. Every part of this one, and every line of the offer list below it, is this skill's own construction.

**The trigger.** A stated point after which still-looking is escalated. **Do not invent a number of minutes.** None is published anywhere, and a plausible figure here would be fabrication wearing a benchmark's clothes.

Anchor it to a clock the day already contains instead:

- the end of the pitch round
- the first meal service
- the first workshop break

Write down what it actually took: your own next edition is the only place a real number will ever come from.

**The owner.** Named, and someone who can decide rather than only relay. Asking a team to take a fifth person under a cap of four is a decision, and it has to belong to somebody.

**The ask.** Made to a team, in private, with a specific person and a specific contribution named - not a room-wide appeal. A public request to absorb someone identifies them as unwanted in front of everyone, which costs more than it buys and cannot be taken back.

**The floor.** The escalation ends with the participant choosing among real options, never with them being placed. Being assigned to a team that did not want them is worse than working alone, and a protocol that cannot end in "I'll work solo, and that's fine" is coercive rather than supportive.

## What the escalation may offer, and what it may not

May:

- A place on a team that has agreed, in advance and privately, to take someone.
- A stocked project at the joinable bar, with a named person to ask questions of for the first hour.
- Pairing with a mentor, handed to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@hackathon-mentoring`, which owns that relationship. This skill hands over; it does not build a second mentor channel.
- Solo work, offered as one option among several rather than as the residue.

May not:

- Override a published team-size cap without saying so publicly and applying it to everyone. A quiet exception is a rule change made in favour of one person.
- Alter anything a track's composition fraction depends on. Adding a fifth member who is not a first-timer to a beginners-track team costs that team its track.
- Promise anything about prizes, judging or eligibility. Those belong to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@hackathon-judging` and `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@hackathon-cash-prize`.

## Accessibility overlay

A room-wide pitch round and a facilitated mixer both assume comfort speaking to strangers at volume and processing a fast verbal round. That is an access question entirely independent of the unteamed-outcome question, and it produces unteamed participants for a reason no safety net addresses.

Provide a non-verbal route to the same mechanic - a written project entry read out by the facilitator, a joinable list captured during the round - and take the design of it to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-accessibility-inclusion`, which owns provision depth and the request channel. Do not treat an access need as a matchmaking failure to be swept up later.

## Safeguarding and data overlay

Where minors participate, a sweep is a safeguarding interaction and not a hospitality one: an adult approaching an isolated young person, alone, about their social situation. Apply the event's existing posture rather than improvising - who may hold that conversation, whether it happens in view of others, and what is recorded.

Where nobody is a minor it is still personal information: an observation about a named person's social difficulty, held by volunteers. `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-accessibility-inclusion` already sets a posture for sensitive attendee data; reuse it rather than writing a second one.

## What never gets written down

- A named list of who was alone. Count them; do not roster them.
- Any note that follows a person to a future edition.
- Anything at all in a channel a participant can read.

Aggregate counts are what the next edition needs and are all it needs.

## Escalating out of this skill

If somebody is unteamed because of something another participant did - exclusion, harassment, a team refusing someone on a basis that is not about the work - it stopped being a team-formation problem the moment that happened. Route it to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct`, which owns reporting and enforcement, and do not attempt to resolve it by finding the person another team. This skill handles the ordinary case where nobody did anything wrong and somebody still ended up alone.

## What to measure

All self-set, and only meaningful against your own previous edition.

- How many people registered alone versus as a team.
- How many were still unteamed when the sweep ran. If nobody can state this number, the sweep had no target and the escalation had no trigger.
- What each of them did next: joined a team, took a stocked project, chose solo, or left.
- How long it actually took from the end of the mechanic to the last person having something to do.

The fourth is the number the field does not have. Record it once, and your next edition is the only event you will ever have real evidence for on this question.
